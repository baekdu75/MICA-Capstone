The True Price of Going Electric Electric Vehicles (EV) are often marketed as cleaner and more cost efficient, but the real story depends on several factors. This page shows what actually powers an EV, how electricity and gasoline prices vary by state, and how insurance can change the final math. 1 EV 101: How EVs Work An electric vehicle (EV) uses an electric motor instead of an engine powered by a large battery. That battery has to be charged, just like your phone by plugging into an outlet. But an EV battery is much larger than a phone battery, so it takes longer to charge and uses much more electricity, which is billed in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Most electric vehicles have a battery capacity between 60 and 75 kWh. That’s roughly the same as the combined battery size of about 5,000 iPhones. Fully charging an EV battery with typical residential electricity costs around $10, which is much cheaper than filling up with gasoline. This cost gap is one of the biggest advantages of driving an EV. 2 The Myth of "EVs Are Cleaner" Electric vehicles are often marketed as cleaner to run than gasoline cars. The idea sounds simple: The energy used to charge the vehicle looks green. But in the U.S., a major share of the electricity used to charge EVs still comes from fossil fuels, so in many places the energy behind the plug is not as clean as the marketing suggests. 3 Energy Costs Vary Wildly by State When you look at the data, one pattern becomes immediately clear: electricity and gas prices change dramatically across state lines. Some states offer incredibly cheap electricity, making EV charging a bargain. Others have high electricity rates that diminish the cost advantage significantly. The same volatility applies to gasoline. While everyone experiences price fluctuations at the pump, the baseline price per gallon varies considerably by region due to state taxes, local regulations, and distribution costs. EV insurance tends to run higher than comparable vehicles due to specialized parts, expensive battery repairs, and the need for authentic OEM parts for many repairs. Insurance comparison chart By looking at the average annual insurance rates for each body type among the top 50 models, we can see that EVs consistently cost more to insure than comparable non-electric vehicles of the same body type. The gap between EV and non-EV premiums is large enough that it can wipe out much of the savings from cheaper electricity.
